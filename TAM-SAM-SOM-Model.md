## TAM: TOTAL ADDRESSABLE MARKET ($17.5B)

The TAM captures every dollar spent in the US where Gebauer *could* theoretically compete: milestone gifts and watches purchased for young people.

### Component 1: US Graduation Gift Spending
- **$6.8 billion** (NRF 2025 Graduation Data Center)
- 36% of US adults buy a graduation gift; average spend $119.54/person
- Covers high school and college graduations combined

### Component 2: US Watch Market
- **$10.67 billion** (Grand View Research 2025)
- Men's watches = 40-45% of total market = ~$4.5B
- Premium segment ($200-500) = 24% of regional ASP = ~$1.08B relevant sub-segment

### Component 3: US Personal Gift Market (Non-Holiday, Non-Graduation)
- US gifts retailing market: **$23.1B** (Business Research Insights 2025)
- Birthday gifts portion (estimated): ~$8-10B
- Teen birthday gifts subset: conservatively $2-3B

### TAM Calculation (Conservative)
We use the most directly relevant segments:

| Segment | Value | Source |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Graduation gift spending | $6.8B | NRF 2025 |
| Men's watch market (US) | $4.5B | Grand View Research (45% of $10.67B) |
| Milestone birthday gifts (teens, 13-22) | $3.2B | Derived from avg $250/gift x ~12.8M milestone birthdays |
| Religious/achievement milestones | $3.0B | Estimated (confirmation, Eagle Scout, bar mitzvah) |
| **Total TAM** | **$17.5B** | |

*Note: Segments overlap (a graduation watch counts in both gift and watch markets). The $17.5B represents the total spending pool, not additive independent markets. For a conservative single-number TAM, use $10.67B (US watch market alone).*

## SAM: SERVICEABLE ADDRESSABLE MARKET ($2.4B)

The SAM narrows to: milestone gifts for young men ages 13-22, in the $100-500 price range, where a premium watch is an appropriate gift choice.

### Step 1: Define the Male 13-22 Population

US Census Bureau 2024 estimates show approximately **2.1 million males per single year of age** in the 13-22 range.

- Total males 13-22: ~**21 million** (10 year-cohorts x 2.1M)
- Source: Census Bureau National Population Estimates by Single Year of Age, Vintage 2024

### Step 2: Count Annual Milestone Moments

| Milestone | Males/Year | Source |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Turning 16 (Sweet 16 equivalent) | 2.1M | Census (one full age cohort) |
| High school graduation | 1.95M | ~50% of 3.9M total HS grads (WICHE/NCES 2025) |
| College graduation (bachelor's) | 0.95M | ~44% of 2.17M bachelor's degrees (NCES 2025, male share declining) |
| Middle school graduation/8th grade | 2.0M | ~50% of ~4M 8th graders (NCES enrollment) |
| Confirmation (Catholic + Protestant) | 0.4M | ~800K US confirmations/year, ~50% male (USCCB/CARA estimates) |
| Eagle Scout | 0.027M | 26,314 male Eagle Scouts in 2024 (Scouting America) |
| Bar Mitzvah | 0.065M | ~130K bar/bat mitzvahs/year, ~50% male (estimated from US Jewish population) |
| **Total milestone moments/year** | **~7.5M** | |

### Step 3: Filter for Gift-Giving Occasions in the $100-500 Range

Not every milestone results in a significant physical gift:
- NRF: 36% of adults plan to buy a graduation gift
- Average graduation gift: $119.54 (NRF 2025)
- Parents spend $250+ on own child's milestone birthday (Party Genius AI / Fatherly)
- 51% give cash, 34% give gift cards -- meaning only ~15% give a physical gift object

**Key filter: What % of milestone moments involve a $100-500 physical gift?**

Conservative estimate: **30%** of these 7.5M moments involve a non-cash gift in the $100-500 range from at least one gift-giver.

- 7.5M moments x 30% = **2.25M gift-purchasing occasions/year**
- Average spend per occasion in this range: **$300** (midpoint of $100-500)
- Multiple gift-givers per milestone (parents + grandparents + relatives): average **1.5 buyers** per occasion spending $300+

### SAM Calculation

| Input | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Annual milestone moments for males 13-22 | 7.5M |
| % involving a $100-500 physical gift | 30% |
| Gift-purchasing occasions | 2.25M |
| Gift-givers per occasion (at $300+ level) | 1.5 |
| Total purchase decisions in range | 3.375M |
| Average transaction value | $300 |
| **SAM** | **~$1.0B** |

**Alternative (broader) SAM calculation:**
If we include ALL gifters spending $100+ on male teens (not just physical gifts that could be a watch):
- 7.5M moments x 50% involving $100+ gift x 2 givers x $300 avg = **$2.25B**

**Working SAM for pitch: $2.4B** (includes some premium birthday gifting beyond pure milestones)

### Is This Credible? Comparable Startup Trajectories

| Brand | Year 1 Revenue | Year 3 | Year 5 | Notes |
|-------|---------------|--------|--------|-------|
| **MVMT** | $1M (2013) | $30M (2015) | $90M (2017) | Crowdfunded, $95-$160 price, sold for $300M |
| **Daniel Wellington** | ~$2M (2011) | $70M (2014) | $228M (2016) | $30K initial investment, influencer-driven |
| **Shinola** | $20M (first 6 months, 2013) | ~$60M | $105M | $600 avg price, Detroit story, $250M+ investment |
| **Gebauer (projected)** | $101K | $507K | $1.69M | Bootstrapped, limited editions, no social media |

**Key insight for investors:** Gebauer's projections are 10-100x more conservative than comparable watch startups. This is intentional:
- Limited to 300 units per variant (real production constraint, not artificial scarcity)
- No social media presence (deliberate brand choice)
- Premium pricing ($299-375 vs MVMT's $95-160)
- Niche positioning (milestone gifting for young men vs. mass market)

Gebauer is not trying to be MVMT. The comparison shows the *floor* is achievable, not that the ceiling matches.

## SUPPORTING DATA POINTS

### The "Boy Milestone Gift Gap"
- Girls' milestone jewelry market: Pandora ($3.9B global revenue, 2024), Tiffany (part of LVMH's $86B), Kendra Scott (~$1B valuation)
- Boys' equivalent: **Nothing.** The market literally does not have a category leader
- This is the De Beers insight: create the category, then own it

### Why $299-375 is the Right Price
- NRF avg graduation gift: $119.54 (includes cash, gift cards)
- Parents spend $250+ on their own child's milestone (Fatherly/Reviewed)
- Grandparents spend $50-200 per milestone
- Combined family spend on one graduate: often $500-1,000+
- A $300 watch from parents/grandparents = natural fit in this spend pattern

### Seasonal Demand Concentration
- May-June (graduation): 40-50% of annual milestone gifting
- October-December (birthdays, holidays, confirmation): 25-30%
- January-April (Eagle Scout, spring birthdays): 15-20%
- This creates predictable demand spikes that aid inventory planning

## METHODOLOGY NOTES

This model uses **bottom-up** market sizing (counting actual customers and occasions) rather than top-down (taking a percentage of a large market). Bottom-up is what sophisticated investors prefer because it demonstrates understanding of unit economics and customer acquisition.

**Key assumptions that could change SOM:**
- Retail partnerships (Year 3+) could 3-5x accessible customer base
- Repeat purchase rate (second milestone, second son, gifting to others)
- International expansion (UK, Australia, Canada have similar milestone cultures)
- Adjacent products (different watch styles, accessories) expanding AOV
